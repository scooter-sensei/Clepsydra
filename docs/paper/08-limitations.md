# 8. Limitations and future work

## 8.1 Known limits

- **Two CASes per touch**, one per word (a single 16-byte CAS was slower on the Xeon host [E3]). Hits within one tick skip the word0 CAS; a hot entry's touch is one CAS on its bucket-0 word. A touch 8 or more units past its register's unit (realign: every touch of a key touched less often than every 8 units) moves both words by one CMPXCHG16B on amd64, from the two it read, instead of holding the slot [A33, S52]. The two CASes are ~12 of the ~27 ns hot hit [S25].
- **New keys at design load often need a move.** At 85% load 44% of first touches find both lines full; the move costs a round of 8 parallel misses plus the hand-off (~1 µs median on a DRAM-bound table, p99 2.4–3.5 µs, p99.9 several µs) [S38]. With `Background` the move runs in the placer goroutine: at 85% load 0.16–0.19% of unseen-key inserts take over 2 µs instead of 1.3–3.1%, and p99 is 1.0–1.1 µs instead of 2.1–2.8 µs (90%: 0.21–0.33% instead of 9–11%), against 0.05–0.15% for a 0.5 µs busy loop on the same host. A miss reads one more bit (absent-key lookups 1.08–1.12x) [S45, S46]. On the Ryzen host the p99.9 stays above 2 µs, because the host itself interrupts ~0.1% of microsecond-long calls [S43]. Above ~94% slot load the bounded search starts evicting, and near-full (>94%) inserts cost µs.
- **Hot keys under contention.** Every touch of a hot key writes its entry's line: under Zipf traffic (heaviest key ~9% of calls) six cores reach 3.4x one core, uniform traffic 4.9x [S37].
- **First touches** of a key take a striped lock (256 stripes) so racing writers can't create two slots [R2]. Hits stay lock-free.
- **Counts under concurrency** have two modelled residuals: about 6.6e-10 of counts read one high, ~6e-17 one low ([5 Concurrency](05-concurrency.md#51-counts-under-concurrency)) [A29].
- **Floods past the overflow table.** From about 4.5x the table's slots in heavy keys the overflow table's buckets overflow: the floors cover the keys sharing them, strict limits turn new keys away (96% at 6x), and the offenders' `Count` is lost; `Upper` still covers them. `Floored()` is the alarm [A14]. Without `ConstantTime`, timing reveals seen vs unseen ([6 Security](06-security.md#64-what-is-left)).
- **Count saturation without hot entries.** A register alone holds 31 events per unit (Horizon/24): per key, 1,273 events for a burst and 762 per horizon for steady traffic (exact up to about 725) [D7]. Hot entries make counts exact at every rate tested, to 5,000 events/s, until every entry is in use (`HotRefused`) [A15].
- **Memory.** 16 B per slot, 18.8 B per key at the default 85% load [D6]. Bloom-style APBF and TLBF use a few bits per key but give no counts and no per-query window.
- **No resizing.** Capacity is fixed at construction.
- **Single process.** The clock is monotonic per process. Cross-node use needs synchronized clocks or a merge path. Restarts forget everything; no state is persisted yet.
- **Go limits.** No prefetch intrinsic and no SIMD fingerprint compare. The batch APIs prefetch through a few lines of assembly (`PREFETCHT0` on amd64, `PRFM` on arm64; plain loads elsewhere) [S48].
- **Platforms.** The deterministic results hold for linux/amd64 with Go 1.24.7, and for linux/arm64 under qemu-user; no arm64 hardware was tested, and qemu runs the guest's atomics on the host's, so arm64's memory ordering is untested. 386 builds and passes `-short` in three modes, natively on the amd64 kernel. 32-bit arm builds (vet); nothing has run there (qemu-arm would).
- **Accuracy of the originality claim.** The Learned Cuckoo Filter's full text could not be read, so the claim for per-query windows rests on the works that could ([2 Related work](02-related-work.md)).

## 8.2 Open problems

**Correctness and its checks**

- **Concurrent counts** (A22, A28–A30): no open kind. What is left is A29's two residuals. Closing them exactly needs the count and the identity in one atomic step: a hold per count (lock-like hits), or a 16-byte CAS over both words (E3 measured it slower on the Xeon host; on the Ryzen host E21 did that exactly but cost the count path 7% in the test binaries, not adopted; placement-randomized, its Batch cost, 1.077 [1.058, 1.096], is its own). Realign uses one on amd64 (A33); arm64's CASP is untested, for realign or the count. Where the residual shows: `TestAbandonedMoveKeepsCounts` fails about once in 2,000 runs with real tags, one count high, and with every cold key on a hot key's tag 7 of 1,800 runs at 326a1f9 and the current tree, against 0 of 1,300 for E21. A failing round now names the key and who shares its tag.
- **The eviction/move exclusion** (`moveCount.evicting`) no longer protects histories now that copies are hidden (Relocate.tla holds without it); it saves wasted moves. Dropping it (`no-evict-move-exclusion.patch`) read inserts 0.97–1.01 in the test binaries, but `TestEvictionDuringMoveKeepsHistory` hangs without it (an eviction now waits for a move the test holds open), so it was not adopted. Its one racy failure is the current tree's rate too. Dropping it would need that test rewritten for an eviction that waits.
- **A stash model** (TLA+: evictions, restores, the Background placer, ConstantTime's waiting keys). The put's same-identity rule (an older snapshot never overwrites a newer one) sees only the first way it can use, so a way of the key's identity later in the set survives; in the plain path a key's stash writes hold its claim stripe, the Background placer's don't. A probe (`stash-dup-probe.patch`): in 94.1 M writes under soaks and the concurrent fuzzer, 18,493 left two entries of one identity in a set (2,218 of them in ConstantTime's 3.7 M), every time the other expired (more than a horizon older), never in the wait room, and no get returned an older entry. By the probe's rule the premise holds in these workloads; a model would say whether a live pair can be made.
- **The other direction under concurrency.** The concurrent fuzzer and the soak still check one direction (`Upper` ≥ the truth). A36's second direction for their own keys needs unique identities (tags amplified: fingerprints unique in the case) and a tally, not a failure, for A29's documented high residual; a read of the overflow table racing its writers makes the subtraction of its part inexact, so check only keys with no overflow entry.
- **The concurrent fuzzer's power.** Undo each of A20, A21, A23, A24 and the other guards (claim lock, settle, 80caa55) and time how long it takes to fail (an uncommitted mutation script, `cfmut`). Done for A26's move count and A27 with a straggler goroutine: no failure in 10 minutes either way (their windows are nanoseconds wide).
- **`MaxTick` on the hit and count paths.** A check in every call read 0.991–0.999 of the arm it was measured against (means; upper bounds 1.042, 1.061), so A35 put it only on inserts, realigns and decay steps; judged by the geometric mean over layouts it could go back, covering a clock that passes `MaxTick` with keys already in.

**Speed**

- **Unseen-key insert tail without ConstantTime** (target 11): `Background` brings inserts over 2 µs to 0.16–0.19% at 85% load (S45). The host's own share is 0.05–0.15%, so p99.9 ≤ 2 µs needs a quieter host to show. What is left of the code's share is the waiting path (room write, shared counters; ~0.6 µs p50) and the placer running beside the caller: about 60% of the share beyond the host's floor goes when the same placements run off the caller's timing on its own goroutine (S61). Even then p99.9 reads 1.9–3.0 µs on the Ryzen host (median ~2.1), so removing the placer's effect would bring the target to the host's floor, not under it. Which part of the placer it is is open (its memory traffic read both ways; spinning and the shared wait count are not it, E27). Measure on bare metal.
- **Miss-insert latency** (target 9): 429 ns against 150; it needs measuring on a host with a large L3. On the Ryzen host one DRAM round trip is 189 ns, so a table past the cache can't meet it; inside the cache the insert path costs 340–377 ns (966–1,067 cycles), two-thirds of it the cuckoo search, which a third of steady-state inserts run (S62).
- **Scaling** (target 10): 6 cores give 4.9x uniform and 3.4x Zipf (S37; 5.1x and 3.6x at 6 goroutines, 7.9x and 5.5x at 12, S53). Per-core cells for the hottest entries (LongAdder-style) would remove the last shared CAS, but on the Ryzen host they could give at most 1.47x at 6 goroutines and 1.23x at 12 (S53, under the 1.25x bar set before the reading), and every count of a hot key would sum its cells. The design is written and model-checked (experiment E23) for a host with more physical cores; 16 cores need another host.
- **Placement** (S55, S58): a few percent of sd in cycles/op on the cached hits and the count paths, acting through the front end, in its severe form the op cache handing half the hit's ops to the legacy decoder. Neither 64-byte alignment, PGO nor contiguous hot code removes it (S57, E26). The op-cache set model (`opc3.py`, not committed) predicts the mild layouts, not the severe one: probe the op cache's per-line limit on immediates and displacements (the hit path's 64-bit constants: `mix64` in `alt`), or how long the front end stays on the decoder after a switch; for instance, counters on fa64's layout 15 with one hot function moved by 64 bytes at a time.
- **Readings to redo with `lrab.sh NULL=1 METRIC=cycles/op`**, judged on the geometric mean with the null arm's interval as the floor of the bar: the calibrated dither (`ct-dither-calibrated.patch`) and A33's importer TouchAndCount (1.054 [1.028, 1.079] over layouts, where it runs no A33 instruction).
- **tfbench's speed columns** (not measured): one 1M-key filter serves several columns in turn, so its registers fill as earlier columns' iterations add up, and how many there were depends on how fast those ran; `hit hot` times refused promotions (X36). A fresh filter per column, read as a scorecard change.
- **The batch's lookup pass** costs on tables that fit the cache (0.86x at 1 MB, S48): a size gate for it.
- **Evictions at 97.8% load** come from the bounded cuckoo search (D8). The stash hides them (0 missed), but a deeper or BFS search would also cut stash traffic; measure the insert cost. Near-full (>94%) inserts cost µs; consider a per-line overflow hint so misses skip the alternate line.
- **THP for large tables**: 11–18% faster DRAM operations on the Xeon host (X22); on the Ryzen host, granted huge pages changed insert classes by at most 3% although raw random loads got 22% faster (S42, S43). Host-dependent; not adopted.
- **The hot path**: two CASes per touch (~12 of the ~27 ns hot hit, S25). Option: a Resolution floor that skips the word0 CAS when the timestamp would not change.
- **The string path**: faster hashing (SipHash-1-3 would cut keyed integer hashing by a third, S31).

**Accuracy**

- **Velocity error** 0.0779 against a 0.05 target (target 7): the register's information allows ~0.058 at best (D18). Side information a few more bits could carry reaches 0.058–0.070 (D19): the offset of each bucket's first event, or the exact times of the key's last 2 to 4 events. The 1 s window holds most of the gap, and 41% of its error is keys near 10 events/s, whose spread inside a 2.5 s bucket only finer counts remove. Reaching 0.05 takes about the exact times of the last 8 events, or resolution of 1.25 s or finer near now: tens of bits per key, where the slot has none free (identity and counter bits were kept by decision) and the memory targets leave about 7 bits per key under flood. Options: a wider register for keys whose short windows matter (like hot entries; a side table covers few keys of a full table), or a target that weighs windows by use.

**Security**

- Heavy floods past ~4.5x slots overflow the ghost table's buckets (96% of new keys turned away at 6x; A14), and only `Overflow` moves the point. ~0.3% of new keys read Degraded through fingerprint matches once it is full ([6 Security](06-security.md#64-what-is-left)).
- **ConstantTime's dithered pad end** (S41) needs a clock read cheaper than the dither (128 ns): its spin reads the clock until the pad is over, so a read that costs more quantizes the end to the read's phase again. Under qemu-user a read costs ~566 ns, and `TestCTPadEndIsDithered` fails there; hosts with a slow clock source (HPET or acpi_pm in some VMs: about 0.5–1.5 µs a read) would lose the dither the same way. The calibrated dither (`ct-dither-calibrated.patch`) widens it to three spin iterations where that exceeds 128 ns, and passes there. It was not adopted by its rules: placement-randomized (10 layouts), its two arrangements read 0.993–1.018 and 0.998–1.052 of the base with every interval including 1.00, but upper bounds up to 1.052 and 1.189 against a 1.03 bar that placement's own spread puts out of reach at 10 layouts ([record/decisions.md](../record/decisions.md#constanttime)). Next: decide it by the geometric mean over layouts against the bar, or with the number of layouts sized to the spread. Still to measure: the leak (`TF_CTEXP`) on a slow-clock host.

## 8.3 Directions proposed, not built

- **Reclaimer as a correctness guarantee, which frees bits.** If every line is provably visited once per horizon, timestamps can be truncated to 32 bits (ms) without wraparound aliasing. That frees 14 bits per slot (the timestamp is now 46 bits), enough for a wider fingerprint: up to 7 more fingerprint bits without touching counts. (Corrected: an earlier version said this allowed 6 slots per line; 6 slots need ≤ 85 bits each, and a slot would still need about 113 [X11].) Caveat: the guarantee needs a ticker when traffic is idle, because the inline 1/64 pacing alone isn't enough.
- **`TimeToCount(key, n)`.** The inverse query: "how long did the last n events take?" It detects bursts without choosing a window. It is a cumulative scan over the register in the same line.
- **Hot-key touch elision.** Skip the word0 CAS when `now − last < δ`, and use Dice-style probabilistic increments past a threshold, to cut cache-line ping-pong for heavy hitters (the attackers). Prior-art technique, a new application; the contention benchmark found it not needed so far (E16).
- **Mergeable shards.** Merge two filters with max(ts) plus aligned register sums, for multi-node aggregation. Standard mergeable-summary theory, not new.
- **A wider register for keys whose short windows matter**, like hot entries (§8.2, velocity error).
- **arm64's CASP** for realign (A33).
- **32-bit arm under qemu-arm.**

## 8.4 Decisions left to the user

- Whether `Background` becomes the default (it starts a goroutine, and its placements aren't deterministic).
- Target 7's target, or a target that weighs windows by use: a few more bits per key can't meet 0.05 (D19).
- PGO in deployments: 2–14% faster on the benchmarks with a profile of the deployment's own traffic (S57). A profile in the repository would have the benchmarks time a build users get only with their own profile, so the repository does not ship one.
