# Decisions and rejected ideas

What was decided, what was tried and not adopted, and why. Patches for the experiments that kept code are in `go/verify/experiments/` ([artifact/experiments.md](../artifact/experiments.md)); their deterministic effects are re-run by `verify/experiments.sh`. Where a decision rested on a speed reading, its rule was written before the reading ([artifact/method.md](../artifact/method.md)). Hosts: [README](../README.md#hosts).

## Decided

- **Ghost floors keep the maximum, not the sum** (decided by Priyasis). The stress soak found `Upper` below the true count, unflagged, for a key that left more than one history in a full bucket's floor (0.7% of counts in a single-threaded probe with `SpillMin: 1` and the pool cycling 0.5x–4x capacity). Summing floors removes it (0 low), but light floods then push every floor to 31: a 16x flood of 4-event keys turns away 100% of new keys under a strict limit at the defaults (0.1% before), and of 1-event keys with `SpillMin: 1`. Kept the maximum and narrowed the guarantee ([paper §6.4](../paper/06-security.md#64-what-is-left), "Repeatedly floored keys"); the soak checks that every low answer has a live floor. Patch: `verify/experiments/sum-floors.patch`.
- **Keep the 48-bit counter** (decided by Priyasis): exact heavy-hitter counts over trading counter bits for identity bits. Options measured (more identity bits from the velocity register, 1e9 probes):
  - 46-bit register + 14-bit tag (`register-46.patch`): FPR 1.8e-8 → 7e-9, vel MAE 0.081 → 0.085, per-key count cap per horizon 1000+ → ~876.
  - 44-bit register + 16-bit tag (`register-44.patch`): FPR → 0 seen (~1e-9 expected), vel MAE 0.088, cap ~630.
  - Correction (claims X3): the caps were burst counts at one phase. The current register's 60 s burst reads 770–1,273 depending on phase (46-bit: 638–889; 44-bit: 508–633), and steady traffic reads within 1% only up to 12 events/s (46-bit: 12% low at 10/s; 44-bit: 18–30% low). The ordering between the options holds.
  - Truncated timestamps (needs a guaranteed sweeper when idle) would free up to 7 fingerprint bits without touching counts ([paper §8.3](../paper/08-limitations.md#83-directions-proposed-not-built)).

## Placement and layout

- **Sending displaced entries home** (`homing.patch`): at insert (before using the alternate line) and on hits found in the alternate line. Under churn, keys in their second line drop from 37% to 30% (inserts only) or 33% → 24% (with re-touches), but no measurable hit gain on the L3-resident table, inserts +15 ns, and hits on full tables +18 ns from failed attempts. Reverted (E6, S17).
- **8-slot (128-byte) buckets** (`eight-slot.patch`; also commits 4103013 / 6aa1c1b): 0 misses at 98% load and 3x cheaper near-full inserts, but ~10% slower everywhere else. Reverted. Re-tested on DRAM: near-full inserts 2945 → 464 ns and steady inserts slightly faster, but DRAM hits 208 → 227 ns, absent keys 127 → 200 ns (a bucket is two cache lines; the adjacent-line prefetcher doesn't make the second free — a hypothesis, not measured), saturated inserts 514 → 793 ns, and more false positives (measured 29 vs 18 in 1e9 probes, 1.6x; "~2x" was not measured). Still not worth it (E13, S22). The scorecard row: "wins only above 94% load, ~10% slower elsewhere".
- **Branchless 4-way fingerprint compare in find** (`branchless-find.patch`): hot hits ~10% faster, but cold lookups ~15% slower (the slot index becomes data-dependent, so the CPU can't run ahead on a predicted branch). Reverted (E2, S14).
- **Per-line spill counts** (skip the alternate line when nothing homed at the primary line is displaced; exact under a concurrent audit). Absent lookups ~10% faster, but steady inserts +15% and near-full inserts +15% from the extra counter traffic, and alternate-line hits wait on one more load. Reverted; the displaced bit it needed was kept (changes item 13). Patch: `verify/experiments/spill-counts.patch` (ported to fbe6968) (E9, S20). A per-line overflow hint so misses skip the alternate line is still an option for near-full inserts.
- **Alternate-line prefetch** (plain load and asm PREFETCHT0), two-choice insert: hits got slower. Reverted. Correction (claims X23): re-measured, 6c1f5dd showed no measurable change. The plain-load version is commit 6c1f5dd (reverted in 70546b2); the asm PREFETCHT0 and two-choice variants weren't kept (unverifiable).
- **Issuing all eight relocation candidates' alternate-line loads before the cuckoo search** (`reloc-prefetch.patch`): no measurable change in steady or saturated inserts (the 1M-key benchmark table is mostly L3-resident; misses are cheap) (E8, S19). A later A/B measured removing the committed form 9–10% slower (changes item 25).
- **Prefetching the cuckoo search level by level**: the insert tail got worse, 85% load p99 2.4 → 3.5 µs (S38). Not adopted.
- **Prefetching the search's whole second level** (32 lines) once the first fails, re-checked with interleaved A/B: 2-move inserts 1.4 → 2.4–2.5 µs p50, with or without huge pages. The Ryzen host keeps about 4 misses in flight with 4 KB pages (7 with huge pages; S42), and the sequential search usually stops after a few lines. Loading one candidate's four next lines together does help (adopted, changes item 41).
- **Lock-free duplicate repair** (in-flight marker + lower index wins; `lockfree-dup-repair-70546b2.patch`, HEAD port `lockfree-dup-repair.patch`): cut duplicates 10x but can't be made symmetric (re-run: 16x at 70546b2, 6x for the port; racy). Replaced by the stripe lock (E14).
- **One CMPXCHG16B for both slot words** instead of two 8-byte CASes on the hit path (`cas2-cmpxchg16b.patch`): 19 vs 15 ns on the Xeon host. Reverted (E3, S15).
- **A deeper or BFS cuckoo search**: would also cut stash traffic at 97.8% load, where evictions come from the bounded search (D8); the insert cost is not yet measured (open, [paper §8.2](../paper/08-limitations.md#82-open-problems)).

## The register

- **Piecewise-linear density in the cut velocity bucket** (`piecewise-linear.patch`): slightly worse than uniform (vel MAE 0.0834 vs 0.0809). Reverted (E12).
- **Pure dyadic register** (H/32, widths to 32 units; `dyadic-h32.patch`): same mean MAE as the H/24 geometry but 60s error 0.040 vs 0.024. Re-run on today's estimator: mean 0.0817 vs 0.0809, 60s 0.038 vs 0.024 (E15).
- **The Laplace register** (a negative result) and the linear 6×8 register: [paper §3.7](../paper/03-design.md#the-register-alternatives-explored).
- **Side information near now for target 7** (not built; scored on exact event times by `TestVelocityInformation`, D19): the offset of the newest bucket's first event (2–4 bits) 0.067–0.070, every bucket's (21 bits) 0.065, the exact times of the last 4 events 0.058, the last 8 0.041; wider geometries (H/48 in 8 buckets, 50 bits; H/24 in 9 buckets, 57 bits) 0.064 and 0.070. A few more bits can't reach 0.05, the slot has none free, and the memory targets leave about 7 bits per key under flood, so the next step is the target's own (left to the user).

## Overflow

- **The count-min overflow sketch** (the default from dd30cbc to 5ac63d5): replaced by the ghost table in the same bytes (9314d89), which keeps 2x floods to 1.8% strict collateral instead of 88–90% (claims A11). Kept in `overflow.go` for the experiments. Tried before it, on the sketch: restoring sketch counts into the table (the inflated keys then evicted real ones: a never-evicted offender read 992), plain count-min Degraded (99% of new keys flagged at 1x), noise σ from event totals (ignores that histories arrive in lumps; 18% false Degraded at 4x).

Sketch shapes and policies tried (data in [paper §6.5](../paper/06-security.md#65-evidence-floods)):

| Idea (source) | Result | Why not |
|---|---|---|
| 16-bit counters, Horizon/4 slices (the previous default) | at 7.1 B/key: 152 of 200 offenders recovered at a 1x heavy flood, 99.8% strict collateral at 2x | the 8-bit, Horizon/2 shape does better on both (180, 89.7%) at 4.7 B/key |
| 3 rows instead of 4 | 16-bit at 5.3 B/key: same as 4 rows at 1x, slightly worse Degraded false-positive rate (0.4%) | the 8-bit 4-row shape is smaller and better |
| Wider sketch, slots/2 (9.4 B/key once allocated) | 200 of 200 offenders recovered at 1x, 49% collateral at 2x heavy (was ~90%) | it is only a setting: `Overflow: slots/2`. The default stayed at slots/4 to keep the attack-time memory small |
| Spill threshold 2 or 8 | 2 stops only one-event floods. 8 also stops 2- and 4-event floods, but a key can then lose 7 events per eviction | 4 balances the two; it is configurable |
| Escalating sketch: allocate a 2× wider layer when the noise passes a bound (in the spirit of SALSA's counter merging) | not run. Old layers keep their noise until they age out, so for the same peak memory it can't beat allocating the full width at once | lazy allocation already gives the "pay only under attack" part |
| LSB sharing / late merging of saturated counters ([Resiliency of Sketch-based Security](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.11777)) | not run. It targets pollution that saturates small counters; the 8-bit counters saturate only in floods past 4x, where the noise has already swamped the counts | no gain where it matters |
| Cold Filter's two-layer prefilter ([Zhou et al., SIGMOD 2018](https://minlanyu.seas.harvard.edu/writeup/sigmod18.pdf)) | `SpillMin` is the cheap version of its idea: keep cold items out of the counting sketch | a second counting layer would add memory and writes for little more than `SpillMin` gives |
| Ghost table with `SpillMin` 1 (keep every history, so dedup of one-event keys survives floods) | collateral 0.1% at 1x and on light floods (vs 0.0%); saturated inserts 1.30x (S35) | the insert cost breaks the no-extra-cost constraint. It is one setting (`SpillMin: 1`) for callers who need it |
| Wider ghost table, slots/2 (9.4 B/key under attack) | 3x flood: 188 recovered, 2.5% collateral; 4x: 44% | it is only a setting (`Overflow`); the default keeps the attack-time memory |
| Narrower ghost table, slots/8 (2.4 B/key) | 2x: 77.8% collateral, still better than the full-size sketch | the default size keeps 2x floods cheap |
| Probabilistic `Upper` from the sketch (count-mean-min + z·σ instead of the row minimum) | not run: at a 2x flood σ ≈ 40 events per counter, so the bound sits near 200 for a limit of 20 | noise from heavy lumps can't be subtracted; only per-key memory (the ghost table) removes it |
| Sub-bucket floors (several floors per bucket, by fingerprint bits), first considered | not run then: they would cost entries, and past 3x every floor rises anyway | later built without costing entries (three floors in entry 0's place, 3603076) |

- **Three floors per bucket, re-judged**: at the defaults no floor rises up to 4x, so the floors change nothing there (two /1.8 at 6x: 99.3% → 96.0% turned away, both past the cliff). With less overflow memory they halve the cliff (two /2 at 4x: 40.8% → 20.0%; one bucket /4 at 3x: 89.2% → 78.6%; `flood-sizing.txt`). Kept: they cost no entries (they live in entry 0's place) and widen the margin for smaller `Overflow` settings.
- **Heavy floods of 2x slots or more saturated any sketch of affordable size**: `Upper` stayed safe, but strict limits then turned away everyone, and a wider `Overflow` moved that point linearly. With the first ghost table (one bucket per history) floods of 3x the slots or more still overflowed its buckets: their floors then covered every key's `Upper`, and the offenders' `Count` was gone. The two-bucket table moved the point to ~4.5x (changes items 25, 32); past it, only size helps ([paper §6.4](../paper/06-security.md#64-what-is-left)).

## Hot entries and scaling

- **Hot-key contention** (a check, not a change; `contention-bench.patch`): 4 goroutines on 1 key run at 10 ns/op aggregate, 16 keys at 16 ns, 16K keys at 22 ns. Same-tick touches skip the word0 CAS and full registers skip the word1 CAS, so a bot storm doesn't ping-pong the line. Touch elision isn't needed (E16, S23).

### Scaling

**Per-core cells for hot entries** (experiment E23, `hot-cells.patch`; not adopted). Their ceiling, measured first with a hot entry's add returning without its CAS (`hot-add-ceiling.patch`), was 1.47x Zipf throughput at 6 goroutines and 1.23x at 12, under the 1.25x bar written before the reading, so the cells were not measured ([paper §7.4](../paper/07-evaluation.md#throughput-and-scaling), S53). The design was written and checked before the reading, for a host with more physical cores:

- An add whose CAS of bucket 0 fails twice takes the entry's lock path, which gives the entry a block of 8 cells (one line each, from a pool of 32 blocks in a lock-free bitmap: the lock path holds the entry's lock, and `hotAlloc`'s reclaim takes entry locks under the table's mutex). Later adds pick a cell at random. Each cell's word is bucket 0's protocol (count, closed bit, generation): a writer's lock closes bucket 0 and each cell before it reads or changes anything, `load` sums them, `store` folds them into bucket 0, and unlock reopens each with the next generation. An add loads its cell's word, then the owner, the epoch and the entry's block again, then CASes. The block goes back when the entry is freed or its unit advances.
- `HotCells.tla` (HotEntry's adders and writers, a block, and another entry J that may take the block once it is free; in `go/verify/tla/experiments/`): no error in 12 configurations (advance, reclaim or no writer; the block held or free at the start; two adders at the entry's unit, or one there and one at the next unit), up to 11.8 M distinct states. Each guard undone breaks an invariant: cells left open under the lock, reopened without a new generation, or attached without being zeroed (KExact, JExact), and the add not loading the block again, which an adder at the entry's new unit needs once an advance gave the block back (KExact). Attach's own close is redundant (unlock reopens with a new generation either way).
- A test (`cells_test.go`: 12 goroutines, 100,000 touches each of one hot key across three unit boundaries) reads the exact count 3 of 3, and 5 of 5 about 3% low with the cells left open under the lock.

## ConstantTime

| Idea (source) | Result | Why not |
|---|---|---|
| **Sleep to the pad** instead of spinning | `time.Sleep` on the Xeon host never woke in under ~1.1 ms, and every wake-up cost ~64 µs of CPU | slower and costlier than spinning 3 µs |
| **Adaptive pad** that doubles on every overrun and halves after quiet runs ([Askarov, Zhang, Myers, CCS 2010](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/andru/papers/timing.html)) | the same leak as a fixed pad, but one GC pause pushed the pad to 128–512 µs and it stayed there | a fixed pad is cheaper and predictable |
| **Yield while padded** (`runtime.Gosched`) | 16 goroutines on 4 cores: 1.31 M ops/s vs 1.10 spinning, but each call's median latency went from 3.1 µs to 11 µs, and KS D rose from 0.031 to 0.063 | a little more throughput for 3.5× the latency and twice the leak |
| Spin 10 µs | KS D 0.011–0.016 vs 0.014–0.023 at 3 µs | 3.3× the CPU for a marginal gain |
| **Shadow work**: each `ConstantTime` path also runs the other's work read-only (an unseen `Count` estimates a neighbour's register; a seen `Touch` walks the insert search without writing) | unpadded D: `Count` 0.31 → 0.07–0.10, `Touch` 0.43 → 0.09–0.13. With `Count`'s tracked pad: D 0.005 vs 0.007–0.012, about the same cost. For `Touch` the walk doubles the seen side's work and its p99 rose to 4–7 µs, so a 1.5 µs pad still leaked D 0.035–0.08 | `Count`'s tracked pad gets the same leak without it; for `Touch` it costs more than the fixed pad. Patch: `ct-shadow-and-prefetch-toggle.patch` |
| `AutoPad` for `Touch` too | settles at 3–5 µs depending on the run, D 0.009–0.014 | no cheaper than a fixed 3 µs on the Xeon host; kept as an option (no per-host number to pick) |
| Fixed 1 µs pad for both | `Count` D 0.012–0.018, `Touch` D 0.14–0.17 | `Touch`'s inserts ran past it (before the waiting stash) |
| Deferring evictions and relocations off the call path (insert into the stash, relocate later) | not run at first: a key waiting in the stash would make its next touch take the slow path, which is itself a seen-key signal, and it needs a background worker | later done in ConstantTime only, where every call reads its stash set anyway (changes item 34) |
| Capping the cuckoo search budget of ConstantTime placements | it caused extra evictions, which shed light histories | not adopted |
| Placing waiting keys in the pad left over after a call's own work, instead of before it | the leftover depends on the caller's key, so the leak came back, 56% correct guesses even at a 3 µs pad (S36) | placement runs before the call's own work |

It is to be decided by the geometric mean over layouts ([paper §8.2](../paper/08-limitations.md#82-open-problems)).

## Background placement

How `Config.Background` reached its design (claims E17–E19, A19):

- **First design** (E17, `background-stripe-placer-c4c58bb.patch`): waiting keys in the eviction stash, the placer holding the key's claim stripe for its whole search. p99 at 85% fell to 1.5 µs, but 95% got worse (p99.9 50–68 → 140–152 µs): a first touch on the same stripe (1 in 256) waited out the placer's search. It also allocated the stash at design load, 1.8 B per key, which would break target 5's 20 B per key.
- **Wait room and commit without the stripe** (adopted): the key stays in the room during the search, and only the install is guarded. The commit marks the room entry busy, installs the entry's current words and removes it, all counted as a move of the key's stripe. Two races found during development were fixed before the commit ([defects.md](defects.md)).
- **One move inline** (E18, `background-one-move-inline.patch`): it pays for an 8-line search before deciding to wait. Not adopted.
- **One room lock per set** (E19, `background-room-lock-per-set.patch`): within noise, for 256 KB of locks. Not adopted.
- **Background by default**: left to the user (below).
- **Placer variants** (E27, `placer-factors.patch`), to find which part of the placer slows callers (S61): spinning through its idle naps (the tail much worse: p99.9 18 µs), and callers leaving the shared wait count alone, the placer finding work and deciding to exit by the wait bits (within the base's run-to-run spread). Neither recovers what running the placements on the caller's goroutine between calls does (0.10–0.11% over 2 µs against 0.17–0.20%). Not adopted.

## Batches

- **Two-phase batch warm** (`two-phase-warm.patch`): about 9% slower. The extra pass re-scans every primary line, and only 23% of keys are displaced (S47). Huge pages for batches: +5%, host-dependent, not adopted.
- **Prefetch arms** (`row8-batch-c4c58bb.patch`, then `batch-arms.patch`, E20): the arm that touches keys found in their primary line first and the rest last reached +15%, but reorders keys across a batch; the committed batch (ccf23af) keeps each batch's order (changes item 45). E20's other arms, in an importer at 90% load past the last-level cache / on a 1 MB table that fits it: S47's arm 3 as is 1.10x / 0.75x; touching those keys as they are found 1.19x / 0.83x, with the slot handed over 1.26x / 0.84x (both reorder keys); both lines of every key prefetched 1.15x / 0.87x. Variants of the committed arm: loads instead of prefetches for the primaries 1.10x, no alternate-line prefetch 0.94x, no slot handed over 1.14x, 64-key chunks 0.95x at 4 MB. On the cached table the cost is the lookup pass (prefetching the primaries alone: 0.98x there; one prefetch call per chunk instead of per key: no change).

## Memory and the host

- **Transparent huge pages (madvise) for tables ≥ 8 MB** (`thp-madvise.patch`): granted (AnonHugePages matched the table), but no clear change on the 600 MB table either (hit 206–229 vs 196–246 ns). Reverted. Correction (claims X22): re-measured with alternating A/B runs, DRAM hits, absent lookups and batches were 11–18% faster on the Xeon host.
- **2 MiB huge-page mmap for the table**: THP was granted, latency unchanged (but see claims X22). (Same experiment as `thp-madvise.patch`; the explanation offered at the time, "Go heap already benefits or TLB isn't the bottleneck", was a guess.)
- **madvise huge pages for the 2M-key insert-tail table**: within run-to-run noise (S38). Re-checked with the pages granted (AnonHugePages 36,864 kB of 37 MiB): insert classes within 3% (S43), although raw random loads get 22% faster and twice as parallel. With X22 the effect is host-dependent; not adopted.

## Clock and hashing

- **Reciprocal division for the built-in clock and `Ticks`** (`reciprocal-clock.patch`): no change in the string path. The clock read itself (~38 ns in that VM, lfence/rdtscp-ordered) stops later work from overlapping, so the division was hidden (a hypothesis, not tested) (E4, S16).
- **Raw RDTSC clock** (`rdtsc-clock.patch`, benchmarks only): 19 ns vs 37 ns and doesn't block overlap (~20 ns saving on the string path), but needs TSC calibration and trust in invariant TSC across cores and VM migrations. Not done; CoarseClock already removes the clock cost (E5).
- **SipHash-1-3 for `Config.Key`** (as Rust's HashMap uses): 10.1 ns vs 15.6 ns per integer key (−35%). Not adopted: 2-4 is the standard PRF, and the default seeded hash already costs 6 ns. Worth revisiting if keyed throughput matters (S31).

## Build and code layout

- **Every function aligned to 64 bytes** (E24, a linker with `funcAlign` 64; Go has no flag): the cached hit 5% faster, TouchAndCount 3.7% slower, one HitHot layout 20% slower; placement moved, not removed (S57). Not a candidate by its rule (TouchAndCount over 1.02).
- **A PGO build of the benchmarks** (E25): 2–14% faster on all six, HitHot's placement sd 1.8 → 6.1%. A candidate by its rule, not adopted: the benchmarks would time a build users get only with their own profile (written down before the reading). PGO is the user's speedup to take.
- **Hot-path adjacency** (E26, `hot-path-adjacent.patch`): the hit path's `filter.go` functions next to `touch`. Placement's sd rose on all four cached and count benchmarks (HitHot 2.0 → 5.5%), HitHot 4.9% slower, TouchAndCount and VelocityCount 4–5% faster (M9). Not adopted by its rule (half the sd on two of four, no ratio over 1.02).

## Left to the user

- Whether `Background` becomes the default: it starts a goroutine, and its placements aren't deterministic, so the harness and the conformance vectors keep it off.
- Target 7's target (velocity error 0.05), or a target that weighs windows by use: a few more bits per key can't meet it (D19).
- PGO in deployments (S57: 2–14% on the Ryzen host, with a profile of the deployment's own traffic).
