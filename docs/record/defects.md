# Defects found and fixed

Every bug the work found in the filter, in the order of its fix, and the defects found in the checks themselves. Each library bug has a test that fails on the code before its fix and passes after; where the bug's commit can still run that test, `verify/replay.sh fixes` replays it (claim R2). Most of the bugs could make `Upper` read below the true count without a flag, which [the contract](../paper/04-operations.md#42-trust-flags) forbids. How the protocol closes each race is explained in [paper §5](../paper/05-concurrency.md).

### Races the concurrent fuzzer found

The soak and `TestFuzzUpper` give each key one goroutine, so no key was ever read while another goroutine touched it. Four races lived there, all fail-open, each fixed with a deterministic test on a hook and a TLA+ model whose old protocol fails and whose new one is checked exhaustively at small sizes:

| Fixed in | Defect | Effect | Found by | Test, before → after; model |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15bbc28 | A touch taking a waiting key out of the wait room wasn't counted as a move | a read racing it took the key for absent: Count 0, Upper 0 | reading the protocol | `TestReadWhileTouchTakesWaitingKey`: 5 of 5 → 0 of 5; `WaitRoom.tla` (A20) |
| 5e21d7e | A touch 8+ units past a register's unit moved word0, then the register | a second touch in between was aged by the gap or reset away: Upper 1 for 2 events | the concurrent fuzzer's shared keys (6534f47): 59 failing cases in 3 minutes, none after | `TestTouchesRacingAfterGap`: fails at gap 8 → passes gaps 1–30; `RegisterEpoch.tla` (A21) |
| 0c08517 | A relocation's copy was visible before its hand-off | the key was live in two slots: touches counted in one while reads found the other; a copy of words read before an eviction came back and its touches were dropped | the fuzzer's own keys: 8–10 failing cases in 10 minutes at 6534f47, 2 after | `TestReadDuringMoveSeesEveryEvent`, `TestMoveAfterEvictionKeepsTouches`; `Relocate.tla` (A23) |
| 277f9ed | A key's wait took a wait-room way the placer was committing | the placer then removed the newcomer's entry (Upper 0, its next touch afresh) or wrote another key's register into it | a debugging trace (records of every move of a key's history, dumping the failing key's): the key waited, and its next touch found the set's wait bit set and no entry (case 1068) | `TestWaitBesidePlacementKeepsKey`: 5 of 5 → 0 of 5; `RoomWays.tla` (A24) |

The hidden copy also removed code: an abandoned copy now counted nothing, so the fold that added its counts back, and its hot-entry hand-back, are gone. The eviction/move exclusion stays, but the model shows it now saves wasted moves, not histories. Cost: none measurable (inserts and hits 0.99–1.01x).

After those fixes the concurrent fuzzer still failed about once per 7.5 minutes (30 min at 277f9ed: 4,542 cases, 2.98e9 operations, 4 failing cases), in kinds not yet explained: shared keys touched at one tick after their slot went stale (4291: Upper 1 for 5 events); a hot entry one short on a shared key (3490); a key in two slots of one line with Background (1426); an old event missing with Background (4509). Two more had been seen: a hot entry one short under Background (case 1068 after the room fix, with events in the wrong units) and a key's only event gone from table, stash and overflow table (1220). None reproduced on demand: case 3490 ran 1,200 reruns at 277f9ed without failing (with a trace build, it failed in 2 of 3 processes within 60 reruns, but that build's records were overwritten before they were read). Failures concentrated on each goroutine's most-touched keys: pool index 0, in every pool, and the heavy keys (the fuzzer's keys inverted from their hashes); key 0 (`mix64(0) = 0`) is goroutine 0's index 0, not a special hash. The fuzzer stayed out of `./test.sh stress` until these were explained.

| c1cbace | An insert's hold over an expired occupant was not counted as a move | a touch of the occupant at a clock two thirds of a horizon behind, during the hold, put the key in a second slot | reading A26's fix | `TestInsertOverExpiredHoldLaggingTouch`, `TestMoveOverExpiredHoldLaggingTouch`: 5 of 5 → 0 (A27) |

A26's fix holds the slot as install does; the hold opens a window in which a lagging touch's second look misses the slot, so it counts as a move of the occupant's stripe, and the same window was already open for inserts over an expired slot (A27). Every hold of an occupied slot now counts as a move. `SlotHold.tla` (the sweep or another key's insert, the occupant's lagging touch, its re-insert) holds exhaustively as committed; the old sweep breaks NoZombie, the hold uncounted NoDup, no put-back NoStuck. Its first version found a duplicate in the committed protocol, which came from leaving out the claim stripe that first touches take; with it, none. A26 and A27 need callers far behind the newest clock for their second halves, which neither fuzzer made. A straggler mode (`TF_CFUZZ_STRAGGLE=1`) found nothing in 10 minutes, at c1cbace and with either guard undone alike: the windows are nanoseconds wide, and the hook tests are their evidence. Speed: none measurable (S49; X33).

One Background soak of 79 (60 s each, at the A26 tree) answered low: a key's history spilled to the overflow table with 6 of 7 events, the key nowhere else. Not reproduced in 18 plain and 60 traced soaks; the soaks interleaved across 277f9ed, 5638eac and A26 were all clean. It matched the open kind 1220. The concurrent fuzzer at c1cbace, 30 minutes: 5,846 cases, 3.80e9 operations, 9.84e8 answers checked, 1 failing case (4 at 277f9ed): an own key in two slots, one of its three touches at one tick in its alternate line and the rest in its primary (3974; 0 of 1,200 traced reruns). Open then: 3974, 4291, 3490, 1220 and the soak's low.

### The race family: a CAS from a value read earlier

Every one of those open kinds (12598, 3974, the Background soak's low, 4291, 3490, 1220) came from one family of race, in three mechanisms, analysed in [paper §5.2](../paper/05-concurrency.md#52-the-race-family-a-cas-from-a-value-read-earlier):

| Fixed in | Defect | Cases | Claims |
|---|---|---|---|
| 43e707b | A hold counted as a move of the stripe of the key whose word0 it read, not of whoever its CAS takes | 3974, 1220, 1089's ConstantTime kind | A28 |
| 200e5ea | A touch's count, a CAS of word1 alone, landed in a same-tag key handed the slot after the check | 12598, the soak's low | A29 |
| 9464187 | A hot key's slot past the horizon was reset by a CAS from the hot pointer after word0 moved | 4291 (4 of 5 lost), 3490 (1 lost) | A30 |

- A28's first version let an eviction pick a relocation's hidden copy (a busy word1's tag indexed stripe 0xff; `TestEvictionDuringMoveKeepsHistory` hung): busy slots are skipped explicitly.
- A29's first version counted afresh on any change of word0 and so counted a hand-over just after the count twice: the racy regression tests failed 2, 11, 92 and 6 of 300 (plain; 2, 9, 89 and 14 Background), and 3, 9, 125 and 7 rerun, every failure one high; 7903faa, and 7903faa with A28 and A30 alone, 0 of 300. `strayed` decides on the slot's tag ([paper §5.2](../paper/05-concurrency.md#deciding-a-stray-count)).
- 12598's trace, re-read: the evict record precedes the touch record, and the evictor's clock is in the unit of the key's last touch, so a newcomer of the key's tag had a bit-identical register.

### A hot history leaving the stash (0becd6e, A32)

A touch of a hot key moves word0, then adds to the key's entry by identity; if the key's history left the stash between the two (spilled, or overwritten in place as the lightest way of a full set at no weight), the add landed in an entry nothing pointed to ([paper §5.4](../paper/05-concurrency.md#54-hot-entries-leaving-the-stash)). How it was found:

- Testing E21, the concurrent fuzzer's unamplified 10-minute arm at seed 9000 failed case 9787 (a 104-slot table, 17 goroutines), and reruns of the case failed at HEAD as often (4 of 12 batches of 100). A trace build (`verify/experiments/trace-concurrent.patch`) showed the key's eviction and spill between its touch's word0 CAS and its hot add, the add recorded after the spill. The spill half was written and modelled (`HotEntry.tla`, writer `spill`), and 9 batches of 100 reruns passed.
- On a whole-tree export, the spill half failed case 9787 in its second batch (a key Upper 0, nowhere: not in a slot, the stash or the overflow table). A trace build caught it again (batch 11 of 50-run batches; Upper 3 for 4): the key, hot, was moved twice, then evicted into the stash (`stash-put last=613777050 out=0`) between its touch's word0 CAS and its add; its next touch missed the stash (`miss-in src=0`), and no record of the entry leaving existed. Its entry's events were 24 units old, so at the next putter's clock the history weighed nothing, and a put that overwrites the lightest way in place names only itself. A deterministic test from that reading (`TestHotAddAfterStashDropCounts`: a stash of one set; four more evictions, the last taking K's way as the lightest) fails 5 of 5 with the spill half alone, in every mode. Two more trace builds added records of recalls that find nothing (and why) and of puts that overwrite an expired way or spill one; the first failed none of 1,600 runs, and the second, which leaves out the per-touch records, failed in its 17th batch of 50 (Upper 2 for 3): the key, hot, was moved twice, then evicted into the stash between its touch's word0 CAS and its add, its entry's events 20 units old; its next touch's recall found no entry of its identity in the set (`recall-miss why=2`), and no put had overwritten it as expired or spilled it. The one removal left unrecorded was the full set's in-place overwrite at no weight.
- Case 9787's failure rates, 12 batches of 100 reruns (a failure ends its batch): 9464187 4 failing batches, E21's tree 4, 7903faa 11 and c1cbace 10 (both before A28–A30, whose races this case also hits); the spill's half alone failed the second of 3 batches run; 0becd6e 0 of 12. With the per-touch records (`TF_TRACE_ALL=1`) the case failed once in 2,300 traced runs (with A32's spill half), against once in about 200 untraced; without them, once in 1,100.

## The residual high count

A29 leaves one modelled residual that can be seen: a same-tag hand-over just after a count counts the event twice. `TestAbandonedMoveKeepsCounts` checks exact totals of ~77,000 touches per round at 99% load, so it sees it.

- **The failure and its lost logs.** The test failed once in E22's replay and once on the tree without the eviction/move exclusion, and in none of 900 runs of the committed tree (three modes) or 500 reruns on each of E22 and 0becd6e. Both logs were lost, and the second loss showed why: `verify/replay.sh stress` ran its four tests in one directory, so each test's `run.<n>.log` overwrote the one before, and `TestAbandonedMoveKeepsCounts` is third of four. Each test now has its own directory, and a failing run's summary lines are printed. Its sign tells: high is A29's residual, low is a lost count.
- **The fixed replay, on the tree at be15b74**: the soak at 80 per worker, 60 s, 0 unflagged plain and Background with tags shared 8 ways; `verify/replay.sh stress 300` with each test in its own directory: 0, 0, **1**, 0 of 300, and the failing run's lines kept: `TestAbandonedMoveKeepsCounts`, one round of 300, **one count high** (`-1 in all`). So the failure is the committed tree's, and its sign is A29's residual's, not a lost count. Three in about 2,800 runs of the plain test by then.
- **Attributed.** A failing round said only its total; a diagnostic (42176ab) logs each hot key whose count is off, by how much, the hot and cold keys on its tag, and where its history is. A first plan (plain against `TF_DISTINCT_TAGS=1`) was dropped before it ran: distinct tags keep cold keys off hot keys' tags, but the 64 hot keys share one among themselves in about half the rounds, so a high reading there wouldn't have ruled A29 out. On a254c59, one run at a time: plain 0 of 3,000 runs; with every cold key on a hot key's tag (`TF_SHARED_TAGS=1`, every sixth run) 1 of 500, one round one high, on a key whose tag 72 cold keys shared and no hot key: A29's residual through a cold newcomer, as the diagnostic should show. With real tags the test has failed 3 times in about 6,700 runs, each time in `replay.sh stress` (3 of ~2,700 runs there, 0 of ~4,000 run on their own; p ≈ 0.07). The one whose sign was kept read high.
- **Measured where it is most likely.** `TestAbandonedMoveKeepsCounts` with every filler key on one of the 64 hot keys' tags (`TF_SHARED_TAGS`), 1,000 runs each, alternated between 9464187 (`strayed`) and E21 (the count as one CMPXCHG16B): 9464187 failed 4 of 1,000 runs, each one round of 300 one count high; E21 none. With 300 runs each earlier (2 and 0, also one high): 6 of 1,300 against 0 of 1,300 (one-sided Fisher p = 0.015). That is A29's high residual where it is made most likely (a slot handed to a key of the departed key's tag just after a count), in 1.5e-5 of rounds, and E21 removes it, as `BumpABA.tla`'s `cas2` says. The variant moves only the fillers' tags: the 64 hot keys share a tag among themselves in about half the rounds (64·63/2/4,096 = 0.49) in either variant, so the plain test's rate from this residual isn't simply 1/64 of the variant's.
- **Amplified against the first fix.** With every cold key on one of the 64 hot keys' tags (300 runs of 300 rounds): 9464187 2 runs failed, each one round one event high; the first version of A29's fix 46 runs, 55 rounds high (1–3 a run). `strayed` keeps a fresh count only for a newcomer of the counting key's tag, one time in 64 here, which predicts 55 / 64 ≈ 0.9 of those highs to remain; 2 did. With real tags (1 in 4,096) the residual is 64 times rarer still.
