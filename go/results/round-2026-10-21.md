# Round 2026-10-21: correctness under concurrency, the count ceiling, the flood cliff, ConstantTime at 1 µs

Host: AMD Ryzen 5 4600H (6 cores / 12 threads, 4 MB L3 per CCX), WSL2, 4 KB pages, Go 1.24.7. A DRAM access costs ~150–200 ns here. Earlier rounds ran on a 4-vCPU Xeon with 260 MB of L3, so absolute speeds are not comparable with theirs; speed claims in this round are A/B ratios on this host, and absolute times name it.

The round worked down the targets spreadsheet (`temporal-filter-targets.xlsx`): the per-key count ceiling (row 1), the flood cliff (rows 2, 3, 6) and the ConstantTime pad (rows 12, 13), plus the soak's unflagged low answers, which had to be closed before any claim about `Upper` could stand.

## Bugs found and fixed

Five bugs, each with a test that fails on the code before its fix (`verify/replay.sh fixes`, claims R2). Each could make `Upper` read below the true count without a flag, which the contract forbids.

Soak campaigns of 60 s runs: 15 of 20 clean at 5524228, 9 of 12 with the handshake alone, 20 of 20 with both fixes, 20 of 20 at f304277 (A12, A16).

## Tried, not adopted

| Idea | Result |
|---|---|
| Prefetching the cuckoo search level by level | the insert tail got worse: 85% load p99 2.4 → 3.5 µs (S38) |
| madvise huge pages for the insert-tail table | within run-to-run noise (S38); on the Xeon's DRAM table it was 11–18% faster (X22), so host-dependent |
| Capping the cuckoo search budget of ConstantTime placements | extra evictions, which shed light histories |
| Placing waiting keys in the pad left over after a call's own work | the leftover depends on the caller's key: 56% correct guesses even at a 3 µs pad. Placement runs before the call's own work |
| Three floors per bucket, re-judged | no effect at the defaults up to 4x (no floor rises); with less overflow memory they halve the cliff (two buckets /2 at 4x: 40.8% → 20.0% turned away). Kept: they cost no entries |

The 2026-10-09 round declined "deferring evictions and relocations off the call path" because a key waiting in the stash would make its next touch take a slow path. b798b1f does defer them, but only in ConstantTime, where every call reads its stash set anyway, so a waiting key costs its next touch nothing extra.

## After the records (2026-10-22)

A fuzzer of the `Upper` contract (`TestFuzzUpper`, claims A18) runs seeded single-goroutine cases: a random configuration (table, stash, overflow and hot sizes, horizon, ConstantTime), load from idle to 64x capacity per horizon, and callers' clocks lagging by up to two units, checked against an exact model on every Count and Touch.

- Its first run was a power check: 343 cases on a tree with 2890196's ghost-clock fix undone, of which 36 failed. Twelve of them were replayed on unmodified 4b31a89, and nine still failed: five with the overflow table off and four with it on. Two causes. With the overflow table off, a key that came back after its history was lost read low without a flag. `Degraded` there covers keys not held, so this is documented behaviour that DESIGN's `Upper` statement had left out (X32; now documented). The other was a real bug in four cases, fixed in e9ceb3b: a key restored from the stash by a caller whose clock was behind its last touch got the older clock as its last touch, and later windows starting between the two read nothing. The trace build showed the mechanism in each case. The soak campaigns had not hit it.
- After the fix: 1.004e10 operations over 8,549 configurations, 2.6e9 answers checked, 0 violations (34 min, 4.9 M ops/s on 12 threads). Row 3 is met.
- Power check: with 2890196's ghost-clock fix undone, it fails 6 of 310 cases in 3.3e8 operations.
- `./test.sh regression --full` passes on the records commit (ee76cab), in a clone with full history.

## Background placement (2026-10-22, second session)

The session began with the checks the first one left at HEAD (dd18c6e): the harness replay (the 47 recorded blocks byte for byte, plus five new ones, 4a8aa22 included), a soak campaign (20 of 20, 10 of 10 with `TF_CT=1`) and a 1e9 fuzz run (0 violations); commit 13d97fd, claims A12, A18.

### Row 11: cuckoo searches off the call path

At 85% load 44% of unseen-key inserts find both lines full. A move costs dependent DRAM misses (~189 ns each, about four in flight on this host), and the deeper ones make the tail: 1.3–3.1% of inserts take over 2 µs (S45). `Config.Background` (off by default) takes the search off the call path. A first touch whose lines are full waits in a small **wait room** (one entry per 128 slots, 0.2 B per slot, allocated on the first wait), where its touches and counts find it. A placer goroutine, which runs while keys wait, places it by path or eviction. A lookup that misses the table reads one bit of its wait-room set.

| | Background off | Background on |
|---|---|---|
| 85% load, inserts over 2 µs | 1.27 / 1.59 / 3.12% | 0.16 / 0.18 / 0.19% |
| 85% load, p99 | 2.1–2.8 µs | 1.01–1.09 µs |
| 90% load, inserts over 2 µs | 9.2–11.0% | 0.21–0.33% |
| 95% load, inserts over 2 µs | 52–54% | 16.0–16.4% |

The host takes the CPU away ~1,070 times a second (S43): a 0.5 µs busy loop timed the same way runs past 2 µs in 0.05–0.15% of calls, and a 1 µs loop in 0.12–0.16% (`TestHostFloor`). So p99.9 ≤ 2 µs can't be shown on this host for an operation near 1 µs; with Background the code's own share over 2 µs is ~0.1% at 85% and 90% load. Costs (S46): with Background off the default paths read 0.94–1.02 of 981cd70; with it on, absent-key lookups 1.08–1.12 (the wait-room bit), the caller's inserts 0.52–0.94.

How it got there (claims E17–E19, A19):

- **First design** (E17): waiting keys in the eviction stash, the placer holding the key's claim stripe for its whole search. p99 at 85% fell to 1.5 µs, but 95% got worse (p99.9 50–68 → 140–152 µs): a first touch on the same stripe (1 in 256) waited out the placer's search. It also allocated the stash at design load, 1.8 B per key, which would break row 5's 20 B per key.
- **Wait room and commit without the stripe**: the key stays in the room during the search, and only the install is guarded. The commit marks the room entry busy, installs the entry's current words and removes it, all counted as a move of the key's stripe.
- **Two races found during development**, both fixed before the commit, and both caught again by the tests on a mutation (A19):
  - A first touch that missed the table, then the room, around a commit inserted the key a second time (a key read 7 of 20 events). `TestStashConcurrentEvictions` under `TF_BG=1` found it. Lookups now check the stripe's move counts across both reads.
  - A touch that found its key's room entry expired (under a lagging clock) left it there, and the placer installed it beside the fresh one. The fuzzer found it (case 54). The touch now removes it.
- **A loss recorded early** (c12f98b, also a fix without Background): the placer places a key at the clock of its last touch, which lags when placement does. With the overflow table off, a history it dropped counted as lost from that stale clock, so a returning key could read low without `Degraded` (fuzz case 303). A loss now counts from the history's newest event.
- **Tried, not adopted**:
  - One move inline (E18): it pays for an 8-line search before deciding to wait.
  - One room lock per set (E19): within noise, for 256 KB of locks.
- 981cd70, found on the way: a ConstantTime key that only waited for a slot was reported `Restored` when its next touch installed it.

Checks on the committed code:
- sanity (now with `TF_BG=1`) and regression pass; the race-detector suites pass plain and with `TF_BG=1`.
- Soak: 10 of 10 runs with `TF_BG=1` (64–70 M counts checked per run) and 10 of 10 plain (57–68 M).
- Fuzz: 1.03e9 operations, 873 cases, 177 of them with Background (the placer at seeded points), 0 violations.
- `TestBackgroundConcurrentCounts`: 3 of 3 runs clean.

## Concurrency (2026-10-22, third and fourth sessions)

The soak and `TestFuzzUpper` give each key one goroutine, so no key was ever read while another goroutine touched it. Four races lived there, all fail-open (unflagged `Upper` below the truth), each now fixed with a deterministic test on a hook and a TLA+ model whose old protocol fails and whose new one is checked exhaustively at small sizes (`verify/tla.sh`, claims A20–A24).

| Commit | Bug | Effect | Test, before → after; model |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15bbc28 | A touch taking a waiting key out of the wait room wasn't counted as a move | a read racing it took the key for absent: Count 0, Upper 0 | `TestReadWhileTouchTakesWaitingKey`: 5 of 5 → 0 of 5; `WaitRoom.tla` (A20) |
| 5e21d7e | A touch 8+ units past a register's unit moved word0, then the register | a second touch in between was aged by the gap or reset away: Upper 1 for 2 events | `TestTouchesRacingAfterGap`: fails at gap 8 → passes gaps 1–30; `RegisterEpoch.tla` (A21) |
| 0c08517 | A relocation's copy was visible before its hand-off | the key was live in two slots: touches counted in one while reads found the other; a copy of words read before an eviction came back and its touches were dropped | `TestReadDuringMoveSeesEveryEvent`, `TestMoveAfterEvictionKeepsTouches`; `Relocate.tla` (A23) |
| 277f9ed | A key's wait took a wait-room way the placer was committing | the placer then removed the newcomer's entry (Upper 0, its next touch afresh) or wrote another key's register into it | `TestWaitBesidePlacementKeepsKey`: 5 of 5 → 0 of 5; `RoomWays.tla` (A24) |

How they were found:
- 15bbc28 by reading the protocol.
- 5e21d7e by the concurrent fuzzer's shared keys (`TestFuzzConcurrent`, 6534f47, A22): 59 failing cases in 3 minutes, none after.
- A23 and A24 by its own keys: 8–10 failing cases in 10 minutes at 6534f47, 2 after A23 (A24's kind and one other). A24's mechanism came from a debugging trace (a scratch build that records every move of a key's history and dumps the failing key's records): the key waited, and its next touch found the set's wait bit set and no entry.

The hidden copy also removed code: an abandoned copy now counted nothing, so the fold that added its counts back, and its hot-entry hand-back, are gone. The eviction/move exclusion stays, but the model shows it now saves wasted moves, not histories. Cost: none measurable (inserts and hits 0.99–1.01x).

Open (A22): after both fixes the concurrent fuzzer still fails about once per 7.5 minutes (30 min at 277f9ed: 4,542 cases, 2.98e9 operations, 4 failing cases), in kinds not yet explained:
- shared keys touched at one tick after their slot went stale (4291: Upper 1 for 5 events);
- a hot entry one short on a shared key (3490);
- a key in two slots of one line with Background (1426);
- an old event missing with Background (4509).

Two more seen before: a hot entry one short under Background (case 1068 after the room fix, with events in the wrong units) and a key's only event gone from table, stash and overflow table (1220). None reproduces on demand: case 3490 ran 1,200 reruns at 277f9ed without failing. Failures concentrate on each goroutine's most-touched keys: pool index 0, in every pool, and the heavy keys (the fuzzer's keys inverted from their hashes). Key 0 (`mix64(0) = 0`) is goroutine 0's index 0, not a special hash. The fuzzer stays out of `./test.sh stress` until these are explained.

Also: batches prefetch their chunk's lines and touch each key from the slot found (22735cd, row 8 met: 9.57 → 11.53 M keys/s at 90% load, S48), with ConstantTime batches doing the same work whatever the table holds (7f242b1).

## Concurrency (2026-10-23/24, sixth and seventh sessions): the open kinds explained and fixed

Every open kind (12598, 3974, the Background soak's low, 4291, 3490, 1220) comes from one family of race, in three mechanisms: **a CAS from a value read earlier succeeds on a slot whose occupant has changed, because word1 doesn't identify the occupant.** Two keys with the same 12-bit tag have bit-identical first registers in one unit (tag, the 4 epoch bits, one event), and a holder reads word0 and word1 in two loads. ### Method

- An improbable coincidence can be amplified instead of waited for: forcing keys to share tags (with unique fingerprints, so no two keys share an identity) turned one failure per 30–60 minutes into two a minute, and made each fix's effect measurable in ten minutes. The same amplifier on the racy regression test (cold keys on the hot keys' tags) measures the refined fix B's high residual.
- Two records that each say their CAS succeeded from the same value mean the value came back, not that CAS failed: look for a writer that installs an equal value, including another key's.
- A model that reads two words in one step, or whose holder can only count on the key it models, can't see torn reads or wrong-stripe counts (SlotHold.tla). One that waits on one read and decides on another can find a race the code doesn't have: model the code's loads one for one.
- Check a fix against an exact invariant as well as the contract's bound: the fuzzer and the soak check only `Upper ≥ truth`, so fix B's double count passed them; the racy tests, which count exactly, caught it.
- Prior art: MemC3's optimistic cuckoo hashing (Fan, Andersen, Kaminsky, NSDI 2013) validates lock-free reads with version counters striped by key, but runs one writer under a global lock, so the displacing writer always knows which key it moves. With lock-free, multi-writer hand-overs, the only reliable identity of the key a hold takes is the value its CAS took: hence the stripe by word1's tag (A28).

## Concurrency (2026-10-25/28, eighth to tenth sessions): a hot history leaving the stash (A32), and the 16-byte CAS (E21; E22, adopted as A33)

**A32.** A touch of a hot key moves word0, then adds to the key's entry by the key's identity, not through the slot (one CAS of bucket 0's word, or the entry's lock). If the key is evicted between the two, its history (word0 with the touch's timestamp, word1 the entry's pointer) goes to the stash, and the add still finds the entry the key's: it counts there, and the event goes wherever the history goes. Two ways out of the stash left the entry the key's while nothing pointed to it any more, and the event was gone:

| Exit | Before | After (0becd6e) |
|---|---|---|
| The stash spills the history (to the overflow table; with it off, `Lost`) | `spill` read the entry and left it the key's | `spill` closes the entry under its lock (`hotClose`) and spills that last state; the late add fails, and the touch looks again and inserts the key afresh with the event |
| A full set's put takes the lightest way, which weighs nothing in the horizon | overwritten in place: a hot history whose touch has moved word0 but not yet added weighs nothing when its entry's events are past the horizon | the lightest way always leaves through `spill`, which closes the entry; a history with no events in the horizon is then neither spilled nor counted lost (its touch counts afresh) |

A register's count can't do this: it is a CAS of the slot's word1, which the eviction took, so it fails and the touch looks again.

How it was found:

- **Session 8**, testing E21: the concurrent fuzzer's unamplified 10-minute arm at seed 9000 failed case 9787 (a 104-slot table, 17 goroutines), and reruns of the case failed at HEAD as often (4 of 12 batches of 100). A trace build (`verify/experiments/trace-concurrent.patch`) showed the key's eviction and spill between its touch's word0 CAS and its hot add, the add recorded after the spill. The spill half was written and modelled (`HotEntry.tla`, writer `spill`), and 9 batches of 100 reruns passed before the session stopped.
- **Session 9**: on a whole-tree export, the spill half failed case 9787 in its second batch (a key Upper 0, nowhere: not in a slot, the stash or the overflow table). A trace build caught it again (batch 11 of 50-run batches; Upper 3 for 4): the key, hot, was moved twice, then evicted into the stash (`stash-put last=613777050 out=0`) between its touch's word0 CAS and its add; its next touch missed the stash (`miss-in src=0`), and no record of the entry leaving existed. Its entry's events were 24 units old, so at the next putter's clock the history weighed nothing, and a put that overwrites the lightest way in place names only itself. A deterministic test from that reading (`TestHotAddAfterStashDropCounts`: a stash of one set; four more evictions, the last taking K's way as the lightest) fails 5 of 5 with the spill half alone, in every mode. Two more trace builds added records of recalls that find nothing (and why) and of puts that overwrite an expired way or spill one; the first failed none of 1,600 runs, and the second, which leaves out the per-touch records, failed in its 17th batch of 50 (Upper 2 for 3): the key, hot, was moved twice, then evicted into the stash between its touch's word0 CAS and its add, its entry's events 20 units old; its next touch's recall found no entry of its identity in the set (`recall-miss why=2`), and no put had overwritten it as expired or spilled it. The one removal left unrecorded was the full set's in-place overwrite at no weight.

The model's `spill` writer stands for either exit: the history leaves, then the writer closes the entry and spills its state; without the close (`noSpillClose`) KExact fails.

**Every exit of a hot history**, audited: an eviction moves the pointer to the stash; a relocation moves it with the key; a restore takes it back from the stash; the spill and the full set's lightest way close the entry (A32); a demotion and a reclaim are the entry's own writers, under its lock (A31). The rest drop a history whose last touch is past the horizon at the dropper's clock (the stash's and the wait room's expired ways, the sweep, an insert over an expired occupant, a stale hot slot's reset, A30), whose events no clock up to the newest may count. Closing the entry there would be wrong as well as unneeded: an expired stash way can share its entry with the key's live slot, once the entry is reclaimed and given to the same key again.

### Method

- A fix that passed 9 batches of reruns was incomplete: two mechanisms shared the case, and the reruns had run on an export missing `spec/vectors`, so the suites around them were partial too. Rerun at the same power on a whole-tree export before calling a fix done.
- A trace that records an overwrite only where it names the history leaving (the spill of `out`) misses the overwrite that names no one. List every path that removes an identity, including those that drop it silently, before reading the absence of a record as the absence of an event.
- When a trace's cost hides a race, the trace's last good failure plus a hypothesis can be faster: a hook test of the hypothesis reproduced it in one run.
- A 32-byte pad underestimates placement: the same ~700 bytes in three places moved TouchAndCount from 0.999 to 1.147 in the test binaries (S52). The control for a change's cost on a path it doesn't run is the change's own bytes at its own place, never called.
- Measure a mechanism's ceiling (its cost removed, its counts wrong) before building it: per-core cells could give at most 1.23x at 12 goroutines here (S53).
- Speed runs keep the host to themselves: the speed queue waited out a model check, and checks that shared the host say so in their records.

## Scaling (2026-10-28, tenth session): the ceiling of per-core cells for hot entries (S53)

Row 10 (16 cores, Zipf traffic including hot keys) is a gap: Zipf scales worse than uniform traffic. Under Zipf s 1.1 the top key takes ~12% of calls, and each of its touches is one CAS of its hot entry's bucket-0 word, a line every core writes. Per-core cells (LongAdder's striped counters; per-core private aggregates for heavy hitters, in the parallel-aggregation literature) would spread that add, but every count of a hot key would then sum the cells: a rate limiter's `TouchAndCount` reads on every call.

Before building them, the most they could give: `TestThroughput`'s scaling arm with a hot entry's add returning without its CAS (it counts nothing). Rule, written before the reading: build cells only if that ceiling is ≥ 1.25x at 12 goroutines. Reading (5 rounds alternated; medians): 1.00x at 1 goroutine, 1.17x at 2, 1.27x at 3, 1.34x at 4, 1.47x at 6, 1.30x at 8, 1.23x at 12. Uniform traffic, where nothing is promoted, 0.90–1.02. Below the bar at 12, so the cells weren't measured. The ceiling peaks at one goroutine per physical core (6) and falls on the SMT siblings, where other limits share the cost: on a host with more physical cores the case could differ (row 10's 16 cores).

The design was written and checked before the reading, kept in the session's scratch (`~/vr/s10/scratch/s10/`: `cells.py`, `HotCells.tla`, `zz_cells_test.go`) for that host:

- An add whose CAS of bucket 0 fails twice takes the entry's lock path, which gives the entry a block of 8 cells (one line each, from a pool of 32 blocks in a lock-free bitmap: the lock path holds the entry's lock, and `hotAlloc`'s reclaim takes entry locks under the table's mutex). Later adds pick a cell at random. Each cell's word is bucket 0's protocol (count, closed bit, generation): a writer's lock closes bucket 0 and each cell before it reads or changes anything, `load` sums them, `store` folds them into bucket 0, and unlock reopens each with the next generation. An add loads its cell's word, then the owner, the epoch and the entry's block again, then CASes. The block goes back when the entry is freed or its unit advances.
- `HotCells.tla` (HotEntry's adders and writers, a block, and another entry J that may take the block once it is free): no error in 12 configurations (advance, reclaim or no writer; the block held or free at the start; two adders at the entry's unit, or one there and one at the next unit), up to 11.8 M distinct states. Each guard undone breaks an invariant: cells left open under the lock, reopened without a new generation, or attached without being zeroed (KExact, JExact), and the add not loading the block again, which an adder at the entry's new unit needs once an advance gave the block back (KExact). Attach's own close is redundant (unlock reopens with a new generation either way).
- A test (12 goroutines, 100,000 touches each of one hot key across three unit boundaries) reads the exact count 3 of 3, and 5 of 5 about 3% low with the cells left open under the lock.
