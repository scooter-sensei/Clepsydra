## Findings (this session): the open kinds explained

All six open kinds come from one family, in three mechanisms. **A CAS from a value read earlier succeeds on a slot whose occupant has changed, because word1 doesn't identify the occupant.** Two keys with the same 12-bit tag have bit-identical first registers in one unit (tag, epoch nibble, one event), and holders read word0 and word1 in two loads.

| | Mechanism | Cases | Candidate fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| **H1** (A29) | `bump` checks the slot (word1, then word0), then counts by a CAS of word1 alone. An eviction gives the slot meanwhile to a key with the same tag and a bit-identical register; the CAS counts there, and the key's stashed/spilled history is one short. bump's own comment said "a newcomer with the same tag can't be mistaken for it": true when word0 is read, not at the CAS | 12598 (its evict record precedes the touch record; the evictor's clock …649 is in the unit of the key's last touch …648); the Background soak's low | B: re-read word0 after the CAS; if foreign, count afresh. `promote` allocates its entry, then publishes under a counted hold, checking word0 |
| **H2** (A28) | A hold (insert over a free or expired slot, the sweep, an eviction, a relocation's hand-off) counts on the stripe of the key whose **word0** it read (none over word0 0), but its CAS takes word1 from whoever has it: a key installed between the two loads (torn), or a same-tag key with a bit-identical word1. That key is hidden with nothing counted on its stripe: its touch inserts it again (dup), its read answers absent | 3974 (two slots); 1220 (Count 0, and `whereIs` found no slot: the hold was still on); case 1089's ConstantTime kind | A: move counts striped by word1's tag (`moveOf`); `putOver` counts whenever word1 is nonzero and not busy; busy slots skipped as eviction victims explicitly (the old stripe skipped them implicitly) |
| **H3** (A30) | A hot key's slot past the horizon skipped realign: touch A moved word0, then reset word1 by a CAS from what it read. Touches after A's word0 add to the hot entry (word1 unchanged, so A's CAS succeeds) or demote it (word1 their register, which A resets), and their events are gone | 4291 (4 of 5 lost); 3490 (1 lost, a hot entry) | C: a stale hot slot resets under realign's hold |

The soak's low (a history spilled one short, the key nowhere else) can come from H1, or from H2: when both halves of a duplicated key are evicted, `stashPut`'s same-identity rule (an older snapshot must not overwrite a newer one) keeps one half and drops the other.

## Solutions considered (beyond the candidate)

- **H1:**
  - A hold for every count (exact; two writes per touch; a descheduled holder blocks its key).
  - A 16-byte CAS over both words (exact; E3: 19 vs 15 ns; no Java or Python equivalent).
  - Validating with the tag stripe's move counts (two loads of a contended line per touch).
  - Undoing the stray count by CAS (can take an event from the newcomer: a low; rejected).
  - Installs refusing an equal register (own keys only).
  - More tag bits (the 2026-10-08 decision).
- **H2:**
  - Re-read word0 after word1 (torn reads only; the model shows the ABA remains).
  - Count on both stripes (no gain).
  - Lookups treat their fingerprint over a busy word1 as "look again" (a larger rule change).
- **H3:** close the entry and fold events newer than the reset's decision (same effect, more code). Making the reset conditional on word1 still being the pointer doesn't help: adds leave word1 unchanged.
- **Audit of the family:** every CAS or hold from a value read earlier is covered by A, B or C; the word0 CASes compare the whole word, and the hot entry's demotion runs under the entry's lock with its owner checked.

## Decisions

**Protocol (candidate, pending 1):**
- A hold counts on the stripe of the tag in the word1 its CAS takes. Claim stripes (the first-touch mutexes) stay by fingerprint.
- A count's CAS is checked against word0 after it.
- A stale hot slot resets under realign's hold.

**Method (additions):**
- Amplify an improbable coincidence instead of waiting for it. Forcing keys to share tags, with unique fingerprints so no identities collide, turned one failure per 30–60 minutes into two a minute, and made each fix's effect measurable in ten minutes.
- Two records that each say their CAS succeeded from the same value mean the value came back: look for a writer that installs an equal value, including another key's.
- A model that reads two words in one step, or whose holder can only count on the key it models, can't see torn reads or wrong-stripe counts.
- Run the racy stress (D14) on every candidate: the fuzzer and the soak check only `Upper ≥ truth`, so an over-count passes them.
